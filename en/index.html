How I Debug

How I Debug

I consider the process of getting to a root cause — not just the deliverable — part of what makes technical support good.

Don't fix on a hunch —
observe first, then decide.
It wasn't the GPU that was slowIsolated and measured stuttering in object detection, and traced the cause to the camera's auto-exposure.
Symptom

Real-time object detection video was stuttering.

Approach

Instead of assuming "the model inference must be heavy," measured GPU inference and camera frame delivery independently.

Measurement

Tested each hypothesis separately: USB bandwidth, sensor readout, warm-up time, driver settings, and a dirty lens.

Conclusion

The GPU was using only 1/22 of its capacity — the fix needed wasn't model optimization, it was better lighting.

The servo problem was an OS settingTraced the issue past the wiring and code, into PWM clock management, and resolved it there.
Symptom

A servo connected to a Raspberry Pi wasn't moving to the commanded angle.

Approach

Instead of stopping at wiring and code, investigated the OS's PWM clock management as well.

Measurement

Isolated the default settings and found a hardware clock conflict with the audio driver.

Conclusion

Resolved with a configuration change, and documented the cause and fix in the README to prevent recurrence.

Same panic, a different cause each timeRefused to treat the ESP32 crashes as one bug, and separated them into several independent ones.
Symptom

Repeated crashes during HTTPS communication, always showing the same panic message.

Approach

Before just increasing the stack size, switched to logging remaining stack space and capturing backtraces.

Measurement

Worked through stack monitoring, disabling unneeded features, freeing heap, and backtrace analysis in sequence.

Conclusion

Identified and fixed stack pressure, an object-lifetime issue, and an invalid free during disconnect handling — each a separate bug.

About

Background & Current Interests

My earliest technical work was porting Fantavision — an Apple II animation program — to the PC-9801, building the assembly-language animation-generation engine. From there: translation-supervising and technical-supervising books, embedded systems, industrial demonstrator hardware, mobile apps, and AI agents. Rather than stay in one domain, I've kept doing the same underlying work — understanding complex technology, verifying it, and turning it into something other people can use. Right now I'm especially interested in connecting AI to the OS, devices, and sensors, instead of leaving it confined to a chat window.

01

Connecting AI agents to the physical world

Mechanisms — MCP and similar — that let AI operate devices, OS functions, and business systems.

02

Cloud-independent inference

Running LLMs entirely on-device, designed to reduce dependence on connectivity, accounts, and external APIs.

03

Low-power communication grounded in real measurement

Measuring how power-saving modes actually behave, and designing from those measurements rather than datasheet numbers.
